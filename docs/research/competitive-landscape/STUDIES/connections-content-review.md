# Geography Connections blinded content review

Date: 19 November 2026

## Purpose

Review whether the 24 authored boards feel solvable and fair, whether the group reveals explain the intended connection, and whether decoys create useful deduction rather than an unintended second answer. The code audit proves that each board has four exact authored groups and no known unassigned exact quartet; it cannot establish what a player sees.

This is a small qualitative pilot. It can identify board-specific problems for editorial follow-up, not estimate how all players will perform.

## Participants and coverage

- Recruit 6–8 casual players who have not authored these boards. Record prior familiarity with geography quizzes as a brief self-rating; no names or contact information are needed in the results sheet.
- Each participant plays six Free Play boards: two Easy, two Medium and two Hard. Use the Standard country pool for every board. Rotate difficulty-block order across participants (Easy–Medium–Hard, Medium–Hard–Easy, Hard–Easy–Medium); for a seventh or eighth participant, use the least-used order.
- Across eight participants this gives 48 board observations. Track the visible Atlas set number in the facilitator sheet and spread observations across all 24 sets where possible. Two observations per set is a coverage target, not a guaranteed outcome from random board selection. Note any repeated or unobserved sets and add a short follow-up only if needed.
- Set the difficulty privately in the settings menu before each board. Do not tell participants the intended overall difficulty or show them the answer groups or editorial overlap notes before they play. The on-board tier legend is part of the game experience and should remain visible.

## Session procedure

Allow about 25–35 minutes per participant. Use the game’s existing rules and setup summary. Do not teach geography facts or explain the authored categories. Ask participants to think aloud while they consider possible groups, without requiring a running narration of every tile.

For each board:

1. Start the timer when the 16 countries are visible. Record the Atlas set number for the facilitator only.
2. Ask, “What connections are you considering?” If the participant goes quiet, use only the neutral prompt, “What are you thinking now?” Do not suggest a country, category or grouping.
3. Record each submitted four-country group, elapsed time, whether it was accepted, mistakes used, one-away feedback, and which tier color was revealed. Preserve the participant’s own explanation of why the group belongs together.
4. Let the normal round end. Before discussing the authored answers, ask for a 1–5 rating of overall difficulty (1 very easy, 5 very hard) and fairness (1 very unfair, 5 very fair). Ask whether any other grouping seemed plausible and which countries supported it.
5. After recording the ratings and alternative groupings, ask whether each label and explanation matches what the participant inferred. Rate explanation clarity from 1 (unclear) to 5 (clear). Record any wording that remained unclear or any fact they describe as obscure, arbitrary or overly broad.

After all six boards, ask which board felt most and least satisfying and whether they would replay another set. Keep this separate from the per-board observations.

## Record sheet fields

Use anonymous IDs such as `P01`. Record:

- participant ID, session date, self-rated geography-quiz familiarity, and difficulty-block order;
- Atlas set number, assigned pool, board order, start/end time and completion state;
- submitted groups and solve order, time to each accepted group, wrong submissions, one-away events and revealed tier colors;
- participant reasoning in their own words, other plausible groupings considered, challenge rating, fairness rating, explanation clarity, and replay interest;
- moderator notes about interruptions, settings accidentally opened, hints given, or other departures from the protocol.

Do not collect names, email addresses, demographic details unrelated to the review, or screen/audio recordings. Keep notes in a local file identified only by the participant ID.

## Review and decision

After sessions, compare observations by board and by assigned pool. Read each proposed alternative quartet against its source and the [editorial guide](../../../../src/games/connections/EDITORIAL.md); distinguish a defensible second connection from a broad overlap, a familiar fact that needed more time, or a label that failed to explain the intended answer. Compare perceived challenge and solve order with the authored Easy/Medium/Hard assignment and the revealed group tiers.

Treat a repeated concern as a reason for editorial investigation, not as proof that a board is objectively unfair. Change a board only when the participant explanation points to a defensible content or wording issue. Re-run the existing board audit after any edit. Do not promote the pilot to a difficulty calibration study or claim population-level results.

## Limits

The sample is small and convenience-based. Geography knowledge, puzzle experience and think-aloud behavior may vary. The normal game includes a “one away” clue and color-coded solved tiers, so results describe that complete experience; they do not isolate the intrinsic difficulty of an unseen category. The current Atlas numbering is visible in the game, but intended pool difficulty and answer categories should remain unannounced. No participant sessions have been conducted yet.
