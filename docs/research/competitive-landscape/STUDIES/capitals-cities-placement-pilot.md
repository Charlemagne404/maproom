# Capitals & Cities map-placement first-read pilot

**Status:** protocol prepared; no participants or response data collected. The study evaluates the current City Location format; it does not authorize a new mode or a scoring change.

## Question

Can a new player understand the “place this city” task, use the focused country map to make and submit a location estimate, and learn from the distance and answer reveal? Is the feedback visible long enough, and does the player understand how closer placements affect points?

This is a small qualitative usability pilot. It can identify confusing controls, feedback and individual prompts. It cannot estimate population-wide difficulty, calibrate score bands or prove a learning effect.

## Participants and setup

- Recruit 6–8 casual players unfamiliar with Maproom's Capitals & Cities game. Ask only for a 1–5 self-rating of geography-game familiarity.
- Use City Location Free Play with the normal all-regions pool, 50,000 minimum population and no timer. Complete one normal 10-question set per participant.
- Include at least two desktop mouse users and two mobile touch users if recruitment permits. Use the participant's ordinary device and note viewport/input type; do not treat this small convenience sample as a device comparison.
- Start from a fresh browser profile for each participant. Do not explain the map, the marker, the score bands or the coordinate panel. The task prompt itself should be the only instruction.
- Introduce the session as a test of the game, not the participant's geographic knowledge. Ask the participant to think aloud without requiring narration of every action. Do not coach, correct or name possible interface features.
- Participation is voluntary. Use anonymous IDs such as `P01`. Collect no names, contact details, precise location, audio, video or unrelated demographic details. Stop if a participant wants to stop.
- The score reveal advances after about 1.85 seconds. Do not pause or change the normal game flow; observe whether participants can read it and note any interruption. Ask detailed reveal questions after the set rather than priming each answer.

## Session procedure

Allow about 15–20 minutes.

1. On the setup screen, ask: “What do you expect to do in this activity?” Record the answer verbatim or as a short anonymized note.
2. Start the City Location set. Before the first placement, ask the participant to explain in their own words what the prompt asks them to do. Do not explain the controls.
3. For each of the ten prompts, record the city and country shown, the response-time band (under 15 seconds, 15–45 seconds, over 45 seconds), whether the participant places or moves a marker, whether they find the submit action, and any uncertainty or help request. Record the displayed distance/score and whether they appear to read the answer marker before the next question.
4. Do not point out coordinate entry. Record whether the participant discovers it, opens it or uses it without prompting. If a participant cannot proceed with their chosen input, note the point of failure and provide only the help they request; mark subsequent behavior as assisted.
5. After the set, ask the participant to rate task clarity, map usefulness, confidence in marker placement, score fairness, feedback usefulness and overall difficulty from 1 (very low) to 5 (very high).
6. Ask neutrally: “How do you think points were determined?”, “What did the result tell you?”, “Was there enough time to read it?”, and “What, if anything, would you change before playing another set?” Ask whether they would choose another set. Only after those answers, ask whether they noticed the coordinate panel and what they thought it was for.

## Readout and review rules

- Report the participant count and raw denominator beside every count. Separate direct observations from moderator interpretation. Summarize task paraphrases, completion, requested help, the six ratings, reveal comprehension, coordinate-panel discovery and replay interest.
- Review the prompt or pin/submit affordance if two or more participants independently cannot complete a first placement without help or misunderstand what action submits the answer.
- Review feedback timing if two or more participants miss the distance, score or target reveal because the game advanced. A timing change should preserve the quick round flow and be retested before release.
- Treat score-fairness comments as questions for a broader accuracy audit, not as evidence to move thresholds. A score change needs a defined gameplay reason and objective distance-band checks as well as player observation.
- Consider a separate coordinates-first format only if participants describe a distinct skill they want to practice and the coordinate panel is not just a workaround for an unclear map task. Keep the current map-first format and its coordinate option unchanged unless evidence supports a specific improvement.
- Do not infer general difficulty, learning or device accessibility from this sample. Follow up with dedicated keyboard, screen-reader and physical-device review where relevant.

## Limits

This small convenience sample is descriptive. Participants see different seeded city prompts, so item-level score comparisons are not controlled. The protocol covers map placement only, not capital recall or marked-city identification. It does not validate all city data, score calibration, formal accessibility or long-term replay. No sessions have been conducted.
