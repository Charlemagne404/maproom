# Watershed Match first-read player pilot

**Status:** protocol prepared; no participants or response data collected. Keep the current 60-question pool limited to Free Play; this pilot does not authorize Daily inclusion or broader coverage.

## Question

Can a new player explain what “follow the flow” asks them to choose, use the map and answer labels to make a reasoned selection, and understand the `ToHUC` reveal? Do any question or decoy names feel ambiguous or unfair?

This is a small qualitative pilot. It can find confusing wording, map cues or individual questions for review; it cannot estimate population difficulty, prove fairness or show that the game teaches watershed science.

## Participants and setup

- Recruit 6–8 casual players who did not author or review the question pool. Record only a self-rating of geography-game familiarity from 1 (rarely play) to 5 (often play).
- Use the normal Watershed Match Free Play setup and the bundled pilot as participants would encounter it. Do not explain HUCs, `ToHUC`, downstream flow, map colors, the arrows or how to reason about an answer.
- Let each participant complete two five-question sets, using the normal replay action between sets. The generator samples all three regions in each set. Record the visible source HUC12, sample area, choices, answer and set position so concerns can be traced to a specific item.
- Use the same viewport and input method within a session; record whether it was desktop or touch. Clear saved game state between participants or use a separate browser profile. Do not use screenshots with participant information.
- Participation is voluntary. Use anonymous IDs such as `P01`; collect no names, contact details, precise location, audio, video or unrelated demographic details. Stop if a participant wants to stop.

## Session procedure

Allow about 20–30 minutes. Introduce this as a test of the game, not of the participant. Ask them to think aloud, but do not require narration of every click. Do not correct misconceptions during play.

1. Before the first answer only, ask: “In your own words, what is this task asking you to choose?” Record the participant's words without paraphrasing or clarification.
2. For each question, note its source HUC12, sample area, three displayed choices, selected choice and a rough response-time band: under 15 seconds, 15–45 seconds, or over 45 seconds. After they select but before they check, ask for confidence from 1 (guessing) to 5 (very sure); then let them lock the answer and record whether it matched the stored destination. Do not ask why until after the answer is locked.
3. After the normal reveal, use only the neutral prompt: “What did that result tell you?” Record whether they refer to the named downstream unit, the map, the arrow, or the majority-flow explanation, and whether they think the arrow is an actual river trace.
4. After the first set, ask the participant to rate task clarity, answer fairness, map usefulness and overall difficulty from 1 (very low) to 5 (very high). Ask: “What was hardest to understand?” and “Did any choice feel like it could also be right? Which one, and why?”
5. Let the participant replay without coaching. At the end, ask what they think “the neighboring HUC12 that naturally receives most of this unit’s flow” means, whether the second set felt easier, and whether they would play another set.

If the participant asks for a definition or hint, say: “Please use the game as it appears; I’ll note where it was unclear.” If they still want help, provide it, record what was explained, and treat later answers as coached rather than independent first-read evidence.

## Readout and review rules

- Report the participant count and raw denominator beside every count. Summarize first-read paraphrases, answer accuracy by region and set, confidence, the four ratings, repeated misunderstandings and replay interest. Treat response-time bands as descriptive only.
- Review an item when two or more participants independently flag the same source/decoy combination, or when one participant gives a concrete reason that another option could satisfy the stated `ToHUC` rule. Check the source record and WBD relation before changing any answer or choice.
- Review the prompt or reveal wording if two or more of the first six participants cannot explain what “most of this unit’s flow” means after the reveal, or think the schematic arrow is a river trace. Do not infer that a single confusing session proves a general failure.
- If first-set confusion is common but the second set improves, preserve both observations: learning during the game may be working even if the initial prompt needs revision. Do not change scoring, difficulty or coverage from these observations alone.
- After any content or rule change, rerun the data, unit, production-artifact and focused browser checks; repeat the pilot only for the changed question or wording.

## Limits

This convenience sample is too small for population estimates or statistical claims. Participants see the normal feedback after each answer, so later questions measure a short learning experience as well as first-read usability. The pilot uses only three U.S. sample areas and the current name-similarity shortlist. It does not validate national coverage, river-network navigation, physical touch precision, screen-reader operation or downstream hydrology beyond the stored WBD `ToHUC` records. No sessions have been conducted.
