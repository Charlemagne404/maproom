# WhenTaken and WhereTaken

**Sources:** [WhenTaken](https://whentaken.com/), [WhereTaken](https://wheretaken.com/), and the [official Teuteuf portfolio](https://teuteuf.fr/) · **Accessed:** 19 November 2026 · **Evidence:** public instructions and initial game states; no submitted scored round.

## Documented

WhenTaken's public instructions say to inspect a photo, place a map pin, select or type a year, and submit a guess. The daily interface showed five rounds, a year slider and an interactive map. WhereTaken's official homepage describes a daily game and archive; its game instructions describe inspecting a photo and placing a map pin. Teuteuf lists additional WhereTaken Classic and US modes for country/region and state identification, and WhenTaken Movies for scene location/year.

## Access limit

On entering play, the sites presented consent dialogs including precise geolocation and advertising/identifier processing. No consent was granted and no round was submitted. The photograph or initial map did not provide enough evidence to claim the correct answer, score, hint progression, attribution display, or replay behavior.

## Interpretation and Maproom implication

Photo guessing can combine visual clues with a spatial commitment, and a year slider adds temporal reasoning. It also creates obligations beyond game code: each image needs a durable source, license, attribution, context and local/offline storage plan. Maproom should not reuse competitor images or treat scraped photographs as available. Existing local city data can support a marker-first format without images; this remains a safer candidate if player testing shows a real gap.
