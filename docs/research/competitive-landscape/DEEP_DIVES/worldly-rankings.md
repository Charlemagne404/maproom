# Worldly Games — Rankings

**Sources:** [Rankings practice](https://worldly.games/rankings/practice), [official FAQ](https://worldly.games/faq) · **Accessed:** 19 November 2026 · **Evidence:** one public practice puzzle completed; the live round is one sample, not a difficulty study.

## Observed

The practice setup allowed a random or selected criterion, continent and region. A random setup produced a six-country GDP puzzle ordered highest to lowest. Countries appeared as a six-row board with a drag handle; the live help prompt and FAQ also described tapping one country and another to swap. I used tap-to-swap successfully.

The initial arrangement received **2/6** correct positions. After moving Cambodia above Grenada, the second guess received **4/6**. Swapping Syrian Arab Republic and New Caledonia then solved the board on the third guess, **6/6**. The result showed the displayed GDP figures (Iran $300.3B, Serbia $112.0B, Cambodia $52.4B, Syrian Arab Republic $17.3B, New Caledonia $8.5B, Grenada $1.5B) and a “Solved in 3” summary. These are values shown by the game; the round did not show their source or data year, and they were not independently checked.

After each submission, a guess-history button reported the number of correctly placed countries. The board remained available for another arrangement, and the interface prompted players to tap a prior guess to restore it. I did not test restoring a guess or the drag gesture. The result offered a new practice game or a link to daily puzzles.

## Documented

The FAQ says a round asks players to rank six countries; supports drag-to-reorder and tap-to-swap; allows up to six distinct arrangements; reports the number in the correct position; and lets players revisit earlier guesses. It lists six criteria, including population, area, GDP and population density, as well as northernmost and westernmost point.

## Interpretation

Positional partial credit makes a ranking puzzle iterative: a player can retain known placements while changing uncertain pairs. Restoring a prior guess can also make the feedback easier to inspect. One easy practice puzzle does not establish how often players solve it quickly or whether the six-guess budget feels fair. The live board exposed the countries as generic text in the accessibility tree, while the controls were communicated visually through row handles and a tap-to-swap prompt; keyboard operation was not evaluated.

## Maproom implication

The round confirms that country ranking is an established geography-game format. Maproom already has Latitude Ladder and Population Pyramid Sort, so another summit-ranking mode would need a substantially different player action. Worldly's partial-credit loop is a separate design choice from the ranking subject; any change to Maproom scoring or guess history would need player evidence and an accessibility review.
