# Geography game and web-platform research

Research performed 18 November 2026. The observations below come from the linked game pages and representative browser play where available. They describe visible mechanics, not user-study results or a claim that one product is universally more enjoyable. Recommendations are labeled separately.

### GeoGrid reference review

An anonymous browser review of [GeoGrid](https://www.geogridgame.com/) on 18 November 2026 inspected one live World board and its category atlas. The board used a 3×3 set of country answers where each crossing combines a row and column category; the site also offered a USA board, country autocomplete, a ten-guess budget with an unlimited option, board sharing and live one-versus-one play. Its category atlas spanned flags, physical and political geography, borders, population, economics, names, sports and country facts. In the single cell followed through play, an incorrect Brazil guess used an attempt and a correct Colombia answer showed a 3.4% rarity result. This is a narrow interface observation, not a full audit of its game modes or scoring formula.

Maproom's existing Geography Grid shares the familiar row-by-column intersection structure, so this reference was used to assess clarity and replay variety rather than justify a new game. The refinement adds more documented criteria to Free Play, retains a generated nine-country distinct-answer solution and explains each entered answer against both clues. Its scarcity tiers use the number of valid candidates, not GeoGrid's observed answer-rarity display or a popularity estimate. Daily's established preset pool and puzzle identities remain unchanged. UN M49's official table places North Macedonia in Southern Europe, and the EU's official country list states 27 member states; those primary references inform the expanded criteria: [UN M49 overview](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/overview/), [EU countries](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/eu-countries_en).

### Patterns that informed the new games

- **A single verb makes the round legible.** Guess, trace, route, compare, or order tells a player what to do before they read a long rules panel.
- **Feedback should advance the next decision.** Distance and direction can turn a miss into a better next guess. Latitude Ladder withholds coordinates until checking to protect the initial reasoning step, then provides a precise map reveal.
- **A bounded round gives practice shape.** Six guesses work well for a recognizable silhouette puzzle; five sortable cities provide a short spatial task with an objective score.
- **Replay should change the challenge.** A seeded shuffled river pool and a new city sample let Free Play continue without turning each game into an unbounded list of identical questions.
- **Variety matters more than reskinning.** Maproom already had country identification, silhouettes, neighbor routes, flags and country comparisons. Physical hydrology and ordering city coordinates add different knowledge and actions.
- **External media can be the product's largest technical cost.** Street-view and historic-video games create strong atmosphere, but introduce rights, availability and network constraints. The selected games use the existing map and city data instead.

## Project-specific recommendations and decisions

1. Keep shared infrastructure small: one metadata catalog, one exhaustive lazy component map, shared settings/result/session hooks, and game-specific puzzle modules.
2. Keep optional datasets on demand and validate their schema at the fetch boundary. This avoids making Riverways and Latitude Ladder increase every route's initial payload.
3. Keep answer selection deterministic for a given game seed and round, even in Free Play, so saved progress can restore the same challenge.
4. Keep new games outside `DAILY_GAME_IDS` until a complete deterministic daily identity, compatibility policy and suitable daily difficulty are deliberately designed.
5. Preserve the static client-only model. The selected games need neither accounts nor a backend, and their source data can be licensed and bundled locally.
6. Treat generalized boundaries and gazetteer thresholds as visible limits. A map that looks precise is not a legal boundary product; a cities500-derived file is not a complete world city register.

Riverways and Latitude Ladder were the previous research-selected Free Play games. In the 19 November product-quality mission, Map Projection Match was then selected from the documented idea list because its distinct visual matching task can use the already bundled country geometry and existing `d3-geo` dependency. Its property descriptions follow the [USGS projection-use guidance](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-are-different-map-projections-used), the [USGS map-projection poster](https://store.usgs.gov/assets/mod/storefiles/PDF/16573.pdf), and [D3's projection documentation](https://d3js.org/d3-geo/projection). Equal Earth, Mercator, Equirectangular and Orthographic are shown with the same country shapes and graticule. The prompts explain a common use and a key tradeoff, not a universal recommendation. The new game is implemented as four seeded, resumable Free Play tasks and is deliberately excluded from Daily. Browser self-play can verify its flow, but prompt comprehension, perceived difficulty and replay interest still need player evidence.

## Higher or Lower: source and coverage audit — 19 November 2026

The current game compares population, catalogued land area, and population divided by land area. To check possible additions without changing the bundled data or question logic, I queried five candidate series from the [World Bank Indicators API](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api-documentation), using its exact-year and JSON query parameters described in the [API call guide](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/898581-api-basic-call-structures). Every query used observation year 2023; the returned data metadata said it was last updated 11 November 2026. Results were joined by ISO3 to Maproom's 195 Standard identities and 243 Extended identities (the catalog excluding its 12 special-geographic records).

Coverage counts include every finite, non-null observation. Pair counts are the number of unordered, distinct-country pairs in the global pool that meet Higher or Lower's current ratio bands: Standard is at least 1.25; Close is 1.01 through 1.45. The existing positive-value requirement is applied first. These counts show possible pairs, not how frequently the seeded random generator will select them; continent scope has fewer candidates.

**Recommendation.** Life expectancy is the strongest source candidate for a separate content review: it is positive, covers nearly every Standard identity, and supplies many close pairs. This is a source-readiness finding, not approval to add the metric. A content decision should first establish the geography-learning purpose and ensure the displayed unit and source help players interpret comparisons. GDP per capita is also sourceable but has far fewer close pairs and changes the game toward economic knowledge. Internet use has lower coverage; CO₂ has a more specialized definition and two zero values; signed population growth does not fit the current positive ratio model without a separately designed comparison rule.

The audit also corrected an existing provenance mismatch: population uses the 2023 World Bank series where it has an observation and falls back to catalog population otherwise; catalog area has no observation year. Density inherits those inputs. The result copy now states those sources rather than describing all values as 2023. At the time of the source-readiness audit, no metric, data file, or comparison rule changed; the separate content decision below supersedes that status.

**Content decision — 19 November 2026.** Add life expectancy at birth to Higher or Lower Free Play only. This contributes a human-geography comparison about population outcomes that is distinct from country size and density. The unit and interpretation are explicit: a 2023 estimate in years, describing how long a newborn would live if mortality patterns at birth persisted. It must not be presented as an individual's predicted lifespan or a direct measure of healthcare quality. The World Bank indicator page lists CC BY 4.0 and identifies UN World Population Prospects, national statistical offices and Eurostat as source providers; the API response was last updated 11 November 2026.

The existing ratio bands are a poor fit: 17,506 of 18,721 Standard-pool country pairs fell into the old 1.01–1.45 Close Calls band. For life expectancy only, use absolute differences instead: Close is 1 to less than 4 years, and Standard is at least 4 years. The 2023 sample supplies 3,962 Close and 13,258 Standard pairs in the Standard world pool; every continent has at least 20 pairs in each tier. This supports 10- and 20-question rounds, while 50-question scopes may end early and now disclose the available comparison count. Daily continues to use population, area and density; player clarity and challenge should be gathered before considering the new measure for Daily. ## Distance & Direction: pair calibration — 19 November 2026

I sampled the current `cities-major.json` input (12,405 records, 11,577 after the Free Play current-settlement filter) across Standard and Extended country pools. Five deterministic seeds generated 20 questions per World-pool configuration at the 50,000 and 1 million population thresholds. These generator samples measure available questions and correct-answer position, not player completion or player accuracy; no aggregate player-response data was available for this audit.

The audit showed that the old Close filter for latitude and longitude had only a 0.35° lower bound. In 100-question samples, median latitude gaps reached 20.31° and median longitude gaps reached 82.57°, despite the “Close calls” label. The filter now accepts coordinate gaps from 0.35° to below 4°, matching the Standard threshold of at least 4°. Distance Close calls retain their existing ratio band of 1.01–1.35; Standard remains at least 1.25.

| Measure | Close-sample median gap across pool and population settings | 90th-percentile gap | Correct key shown on left |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Distance | 1.11–1.19 ratio | 1.26–1.31 ratio | 45–51% |
| Latitude | 1.80–2.06° | 3.51–3.64° | 49–55% |
| Longitude | 1.98–2.46° | 3.45–3.69° | 47–60% |

All 12 world-scope samples produced their requested 20 questions. A separate 50-question sample covered 84 pool/region/threshold/measure combinations; six were shorter because the candidate cities could not supply 50 distinct questions. Each short deck was an Oceania round at the 1 million population minimum: both pools produced 13 Close distance, 32 Close latitude, and 4 Close longitude questions. The Extended pool previously exposed an Antarctic scope through South Georgia, but no current settlement cities qualify there; the selector now offers only regions with at least three eligible cities at the selected population threshold. The generator avoids repeating an exact origin plus unordered choice pair, while allowing the same three-city set to form a different comparison from another origin. Finite short rounds explain the available count and suggest broader settings; the existing no-comparisons message handles combinations with no valid pair.

The two-choice input and answer feedback remain unchanged. The pair bands and generator fill are now explicit and reproducible; whether people finish the new close bands or find them appropriately challenging remains a player-research question.

## Research limits

This was a focused product and architecture review, not a formal usability, accessibility or performance study. Sites change their rules and free tiers over time. Some third-party interfaces depend on browser scripts or external media, so their inspected mode set may differ by region or session. The project-specific recommendations above are design judgments based on Maproom's current static architecture and bundled data.
