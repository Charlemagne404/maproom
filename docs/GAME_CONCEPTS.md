# New-game concept backlog

This backlog combines research opportunities with Maproom's existing data, browser-only architecture and current games. It was ranked on 18 November 2026. Scores are comparative product judgments, not user-study measurements.

**Score key:** `O` originality, `R` replay potential, `E` ease to build (5 = lower effort), `D` data readiness, `V` visual and mobile fit, and `X` separation from existing games (5 = little overlap). Scores run from 1 to 5. The mechanic column states both the core rules and the intended enjoyment hook. Rank favors concepts that are distinct, repeatable and buildable with sourceable data.

## Selected concepts

Riverways and Latitude Ladder were the strongest pair because they add distinct actions and disciplines: tracing physical geography and ordering urban coordinates. Both fit existing local data, can produce seeded rounds without a server, and use shared setup, result, persistence and statistics paths. Free Play city games omit GeoNames records for historical, abandoned, destroyed or sectioned places, while daily city generation retains its established data pool. Both new games remain outside Daily while their difficulty and long-term replay patterns are observed.

Watershed Match, Climate Twins and Biomes Mosaic remain proposals, not implementation commitments. Terrain Profile is deferred in its current sorting form because its main player action is not distinct enough. Map Projection Match moved into implementation during the 19 November quality mission after official projection-property review showed that the task could be generated from existing local geometry without new datasets. Keep it outside Daily until players have assessed its prompts, effort and replay value.

### Watershed Match source review — 18 November 2026

HydroBASINS remains the strongest identified source for nested basin polygons with upstream/downstream coding. Its product inherits the HydroSHEDS v1 license, which allows use but sets conditions for software distribution and prohibits standalone data redistribution. Maproom has no end-user license, so HydroBASINS is not bundled. The source is based on 15-arc-second HydroSHEDS data; its documentation warns of substantially lower quality north of 60°N.

A second candidate, Basin90m, was reviewed on 19 November. GFZ's data description labels the dataset CC BY 4.0 and describes 667,629 basins larger than 50 km², each paired with its longest river channel. The published workflow also says HydroBASINS basin boundaries were used to divide the 90 m SRTM DEM into 130 processing segments. The available license notes do not resolve whether distributing Basin90m-derived geometry in software also triggers HydroSHEDS v1 terms; that dependency needs clarification before bundling. Basin90m also stops short of the original game brief: SRTM covers 60°N to 56°S, drainage systems intersecting 60°N were removed, and the listed Basin90m schema contains a feature ID and morphometric fields but no documented parent/child IDs. The authors also caution that false rivers were not manually removed, especially in flat and heavily modified terrain. This is a research candidate, not a cleared source or a license conclusion. The follow-up below evaluates other sources and reframes the gameplay action.

Sources: [HydroBASINS product page](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins), [HydroSHEDS v1 license and technical documentation](https://data.hydrosheds.org/file/technical-documentation/HydroSHEDS_TechDoc_v1_4.pdf), [Basin90m GFZ data description](https://datapub.gfz-potsdam.de/download/10.5880.GFZ.4.6.2023.004-VEWUOn/2023-004_He-et-al_Data_Description_Basin90m_v1.1.pdf), [Basin90m methods and limitations](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/1151/2024/), and [Basin90m data record](https://doi.org/10.5880/GFZ.4.6.2023.004).

### Watershed Match source and gameplay follow-up — 19 November 2026

The public-domain [USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset (WBD)](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS%3A0101bc32-916e-481d-8654-db7f8509fd0c) and its [official 2024 snapshot](https://water.usgs.gov/catalog/datasets/8dcb8081-66d3-4aff-8795-3d67e89a3162/) provide a practical U.S.-only alternative to HydroBASINS. WBD is complete through 12-digit hydrologic units across the United States; HUC codes form a nested hierarchy, and polygon attributes include names, downstream HUCs, type and area. A hydrologic unit represents drainage to an outlet, but one unit can describe only part of a larger river's total upstream drainage. The data supports explicit HUC-to-HUC relationships, not an unqualified claim that every HUC12 is the complete basin of its named river.

The [USGS Mainstem Rivers release, version 3.1](https://www.usgs.gov/data/mainstem-rivers-conterminous-united-states-ver-31-october-2026), is marked CC0 and includes mainstem paths, HUC crosswalks and outlet information. Version 3.1 corrects outlet assignments; its final-WBD outlet package contains 78,275 outlets. Its summarized river geometry is simplified for visualization. This offers enough candidate content for a regional Free Play game, but it is not a ready-made global basin layer. The Mainstems release uses a January 2025 final-WBD snapshot, while the separate official WBD-2024 snapshot is a different point-in-time product. Their HUC coverage and geometry must be aligned before overlaying the two.

Two global alternatives remain less suitable for a lightweight offline game. [MERIT-FullBasin v1.1](https://zenodo.org/records/22895352) has simplified outlines for 340,955 basins of at least 1 km², but its 6.4 GB global archive also contains other layers, its release terms require a choice between CC BY-NC 4.0 and ODbL 1.0, and its upstream index is a separate product. Commercial use requires ODbL and its share-alike terms. Its basin groups at HydroBASINS levels 1–3 also have a separate source dependency. [Lake-TopoCat](https://ihp-wins.unesco.org/en/dataset/lake-topocat-global-lake-drainage-topology-and-catchment-database-hydrolakes-topocat-v1-1) supplies global lake-linked catchments and reaches under CC BY 4.0, but covers about 57% of global land and is not a complete river-basin layer; its HydroLAKES and MERIT Hydro inputs need their own redistribution review.

The original “pin a river into a basin” action is too close to river and natural-feature location games. In one live [MapGuesser Find the Feature](https://mapguesser.app/play/find-the-feature/) round, the prompt asked for Golfe du Lion; after a pin, the game revealed the target outline and reported a 1,627 km miss. Its official page describes 616 natural features, including 188 rivers, in five-round sessions. This was one sea round, so it does not establish how river questions play. It does show that pin placement plus outline-and-distance feedback is already used for physical features.

**Decision:** keep Watershed Match as a candidate, but test a downstream-relationship loop rather than another river-location round. A prompt could show a subwatershed and ask which adjacent HUC or named mainstem receives its drainage, then reveal the linked path. Before implementation, align one pinned USGS WBD/Mainstems snapshot pair, audit names and ambiguous outlet types, measure the simplified bundle size, and confirm the relationship puzzle stays usable by keyboard and touch. No data or game code was added in this review.

### Future Capitals & Cities mode: Blind coordinates

This is a possible mode inside the existing Capitals & Cities game, not a new game or a commitment for the current release. The player sees a coordinate pair while the map is hidden, makes a location identification or estimate, then submits to reveal the map and compare the answer with the target. Existing bundled city coordinates and marker/reveal components make a local implementation feasible without new services or datasets. The answer surface still needs a clear design decision: a name answer can identify a place from coordinates, while a marker estimate needs a deliberately chosen blank plotting surface if no map may be shown. Any implementation would need hidden-map state, keyboard focus, touch, reduced-motion and reveal behavior coverage, plus a calibrated scoring rule that accounts for distance and the coordinate precision shown. It was documented only; the normal city-placement flow remains the active game.
